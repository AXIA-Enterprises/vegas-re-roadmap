Legal & compliance

Nevada licensing law (NRS Chapter 645, administered by the Nevada Real Estate Division) and federal RESPA Section 8, as they apply to a site owner who is not a real-estate licensee.

Not legal advice. This is a planning summary of public statutes and regulator guidance, written by non-lawyers. Statutes are paraphrased except where quoted, and the short quotes come from the linked pages. Laws, regulations and agency guidance change. Before you launch any revenue model, confirm it with a Nevada real-estate attorney and a licensed Nevada broker.

Nevada: NRS Chapter 645

The official text is at leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-645.html. Section links below go to the official chapter anchor or to a section-level mirror that cites it.

SectionWhat it says (paraphrase plus short quotes)Why it matters here
NRS 645.030
"Real estate broker" defined		 A broker is a person who, "for another and for compensation or with the intention or expectation of receiving compensation," does any of the following:
  • sells, buys, rents or leases real estate, or negotiates to do so
  • "lists or solicits prospective purchasers, lessees or renters"
  • collects advance fees through "advance fee listing advertising or other offerings"
 Soliciting buyers for an agent, for compensation can fall inside the broker definition. Charging property owners up front to advertise their property for sale can also fall inside it. The site should sell ad space to licensees, not represent or solicit for them, and should not charge sellers advance fees.
NRS 645.230
Unlicensed activity		 It is unlawful for any person, LLC, partnership, association or corporation "to engage in the business of" acting as a real estate broker, broker-salesperson or salesperson in Nevada "without first obtaining the appropriate license from the Real Estate Division." This is the core prohibition. The owner must not perform broker activity.
NRS 645.235
Administrative fine		 The Commission may fine anyone who knowingly engages in, or assists or offers to assist, unlicensed activity. The maximum is the person's economic benefit from the violation or $5,000, whichever is greater. "Assisting" also creates exposure for agents who take part in a non-compliant scheme. That makes agents cautious buyers, and the compliance story becomes part of the sales pitch.
NRS 645.280
Compensating unlicensed persons (official)		 Subsection 1: a licensed broker, broker-salesperson or salesperson may not give or pay, directly or indirectly, "any part of a commission, compensation or finder's fee" to an unlicensed person for services performed. The exception is a Nevada broker paying a licensed broker of another state. Subsections 2–3: salespersons may be paid only by, and may pay only through, their own broker. This rules out per-closing referral fees, and very likely per-lead "finder's fees," paid to an unlicensed owner. Agent payments for advertising also need to go through, or be approved by, the agent's broker.
NRS 645.315
Advertising		 A licensee's advertisement must include the licensee's license number and the brokerage name. Salespersons may not advertise "solely under the licensee's own name." Advertising must be done under the brokerage's supervision and in its name. Every featured-agent ad, featured listing and agent-sponsored article must show the license number and brokerage name. Build these into the order form.
NRS 645.990
Penalties		 Violating the chapter is a gross misdemeanor for a natural person. A company faces a fine of up to $2,500. Officers who personally participate are liable as individuals. The Administrator may file a complaint in court for NRS 645.230 violations. Unlicensed activity is a criminal exposure, not just a regulatory one.

Nevada Real Estate Division (NRED)

NRED is the licensing and enforcement agency. It takes complaints through its File a Complaint page, and Real Estate Commission case files show it has pursued unlicensed activity under NRS 645.230. For example, see the complaint in Case 2018-1542. No NRED guidance document specifically on website lead generation or referral fees by unlicensed persons was found. Whether NRED has issued informal guidance is to be verified, by asking NRED directly or through counsel.

Federal: RESPA Section 8 (12 U.S.C. 2607) and Regulation X

  • Prohibition. Section 8(a): no person may give or accept "any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to any agreement or understanding" to refer settlement business on a federally related mortgage loan. Section 8(b) bars splitting charges except for services actually performed (12 U.S.C. 2607, Cornell LII).
  • It applies to real-estate agents. Regulation X's definition of "settlement service" includes "Rendering of services by a real estate agent or real estate broker" (12 CFR 1024.2(b)). So whenever a purchase is financed with a federally related mortgage loan, agent referral payments are within RESPA's reach. Whether a given cash deal falls outside it is a question for counsel, and Nevada law applies either way.
  • Exemptions. Section 8(c) allows payments for goods, facilities or services actually furnished. It also allows "cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements" between real estate agents and brokers (12 CFR 1024.14(g)). An unlicensed website owner is not an agent or broker, so the cooperative-brokerage exemption doesn't cover them.
  • Advertising versus referral. The CFPB says a marketing service "is generally targeted at a wide audience" while a referral is directed at a specific person. Advertising payments may be permissible when they are "reasonably related to the market value of the provided services only." Payments "based on the number of referrals received" are a listed example of a violation (CFPB RESPA FAQs).
  • Penalties. Fines up to $10,000 and/or up to one year in prison. Violators are also liable for three times the charge paid for the settlement service (12 U.S.C. 2607(d)).

What an unlicensed site owner can and cannot do

What needs a licensed Nevada broker

  • Receiving any commission, referral or per-closing compensation (NRS 645.280).
  • Being the IDX participant for live MLS listings, and keeping control of the IDX display (LVR/GLVAR form; NAR IDX policy).
  • Handling consumer inquiries in a way that amounts to soliciting buyers or sellers for a particular agent for compensation (NRS 645.030).
