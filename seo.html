SEO, content & links

Why three hubs and not thirteen sites, what to write for each keyword, and how to build authority without breaking search-engine policies.

Build vs. redirect

Three reasons to consolidate:

  1. Demand is concentrated. All measurable volume sits in 11 keywords, 3,770 searches a month in total (sum of the Ahrefs figures on the keyword table). Not one of the redirect or park domains matches a keyword with measurable volume.
  2. Doorway risk. Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse as sites "created to rank for specific, similar search queries," and give the example of "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" (Google spam policies). Several near-identical Summerlin sites would fit that description.
  3. Authority compounds. One site that earns links and content depth for Summerlin will rank more of the cluster than four thin sites would split.

The redirect method follows Google's site-move guidance: server-side 301/308, no chains, kept "generally at least 1 year" (Google Search Central). The per-domain mapping is on the domain table.

On-page standards

  • Every agent ad shows the license number and brokerage name (NRS 645.315).
  • Sponsored articles are labelled "Sponsored Advertising Content" or "Ad" above the headline (FTC), and their links carry rel="sponsored" (Google).
  • IDX listings carry the listing firm "in a reasonably prominent location" (NAR IDX policy).
  • Don't mass-produce near-duplicate area pages. Google's spam policies also cover "scaled content abuse," where "many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings" (Google). Publish an area page only when it has real local substance.

Citations & backlinks plan

Google Business Profile probably isn't available to these sites. To qualify, a business needs a physical location customers can visit or must travel to customers. "lead generation agents for corporations" are named as ineligible, and a rented mailing address doesn't count (Google Business Profile guidelines). Don't create a profile for the hubs unless a real staffed office exists. Plan citations around directories that list publishers and online businesses.

Authority-building tactics

  1. Brand profiles. Set up consistent name and URL profiles for each hub (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram) and in the Nevada directories above. Use the same name everywhere.
  2. Advertiser profile links. Featured agents can link to their profile page from their own sites as an "as featured on" badge. This must never be a condition of buying an ad: a link bought as part of a paid placement is a paid link, and Google's spam policies list link spam.
  3. Original local data. Publish a monthly Summerlin, Henderson and high-rise market note built from public sources, cited properly. Local press and agent blogs link to data they can quote.
  4. A news tracker for proposed Summerlin towers. Keep a factual, sourced page that follows the Summit House filing (Hoodline, Oct 2026) and any later approvals. It is a natural link target while the topic is news, and it builds topical authority for lasvegashighriseliving.com without using the developer's name as a domain.
  5. Community sponsorships. Sponsor local events or organizations that list sponsors with links (to be verified case by case). Treat the link as a side benefit; it's still a sponsorship.
  6. Cross-hub linking. Link the three hubs to each other where it helps readers. Don't use sitewide footer link blocks.
